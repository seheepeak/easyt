# My Real Estate Agent Is Too Cozy for Comfort. What Can I Do?

Investing in real estate is a major financial decision demanding an agent who best represents you. But if they aren’t the right fit, there are steps you can take.

Nov. 7, 2026, 5:01 a.m. ET

**Q: My real estate agent in New York City works very hard, but I would like to switch to another one within the company. The problem? The agent often asks too many probing personal questions about my career and family, and pressures me to share long meals at expensive restaurants. I am a vegan and I’m not interested in taking time to eat with the agent, but I give into the pressure and end up regretting it. I also see this agent around town at random places. How can I stay with this company, but hire another agent?**

**A:** Real estate transactions are major financial decisions, and you should get the representation that suits you.

You might consider giving your agent an opportunity to change their approach by being clear about your preference to keep your relationship strictly business focused. You can do this without being critical.

Consider saying, “I appreciate everything you’ve done so far, but I feel we’re not quite on the same page about how I’d like to approach this process. Could we talk about that?,” said Jacqueline Whitmore, an etiquette expert and founder of the Protocol School of Palm Beach.

If you have decided that you need different representation, be sure to review any agreements that you have signed, and note when they expire, before making any moves.

* * *

* * *

You should still speak with your current agent before going to the company’s managing broker. Take comfort in the fact that your desire for a change isn’t unique. People have personality clashes that can interfere with business, and clients switch brokers all the time, said Josh Doyle, a licensed associate broker with Compass in New York.

“The easiest path forward is to have a direct conversation,” Mr. Doyle said.

This may seem dreadful, but having this conversation could ease your future unexpected encounters with this agent around town. Besides, it is preferable to ghosting them, because they will find out about it anyway.

Be gracious and clear, focusing on your needs, not their shortcomings, and be sure to thank them, said Patricia Rossi, author of “Everyday Etiquette.”

“You don’t have to diminish one person to make a different choice,” Ms. Rossi said.

In your conversation with the managing broker, explain that you want to stay with the company, but your current agent does not fit your communication style and personality, and that you would like to respectfully transition to someone else. Don’t over-explain or manufacture an excuse, and don’t complain about your agent to others in the office.
